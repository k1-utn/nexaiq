# Qualified Canadian Legal Review Required

Nothing in this repository is lawyer-approved. Before any external commercial launch, qualified Canadian counsel must review and approve or advise on:

- Product classification and decision-support boundaries; all repair, safety, OEM, insurer, accuracy, performance, and marketing claims.
- Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, DPA, Acceptable Use Policy, Subprocessor List, Security Overview, Cookie Notice where applicable, Enterprise MSA, Beta/Founding Partner Agreement, and contextual disclaimers.
- Professional-responsibility allocation; warranty/disclaimer, limitation of liability, indemnity, insurance, service availability, beta, termination, dispute, governing-law, and data-return terms.
- Electronic attestation/signature identity, intent, text, authentication evidence, records, jurisdiction, and enforceability.
- Canadian privacy applicability by province and customer type; accountability, purposes, authority/consent, notices, access/correction, complaints, employee privacy, children if relevant, breach duties, and controller/service-provider terminology.
- Cross-border processing and actual residency claims for every host, AI provider, monitoring vendor, payment vendor, subprocessor, backup, and support pathway.
- Retention periods, litigation/legal holds, deletion/export scope, backup deletion behaviour, and contractual/statutory recordkeeping.
- Security commitments, breach-notification duties, incident communications, audit-log positioning, and whether any evidence workflow supports stronger evidentiary claims.
- Authentication policy for pilot and production, including MFA, session lifetime, account recovery, password-reset handling, and leaked-password screening. Supabase leaked-password protection is not enabled in the current free development project and remains a pre-pilot requirement on a supported plan.
- Customer data ownership, permitted use, de-identification, analytics, model improvement/training, feedback rights, confidentiality, and subprocessor change process.
- Employee qualification, training, sign-off, performance-adjacent, and privacy controls; ensure no hidden surveillance or unsupported competency inference.
- OEM and estimating-system licences, subscription terms, uploads, summaries, excerpts, derived data, redistribution, training rights, and proposed provider partnerships.
- Mitchell/CCC/ALLDATA/OEC/ICBC and other integration wording, authorized access methods, API agreements, payer-vs-OEM requirement separation, and no-affiliation statements.
- Copyright, database rights, trademarks, nexaIQ name/domain clearance, patent strategy if any, open-source licence obligations, and third-party notices.
- Corporation, jurisdiction, share structure, founder arrangements, IP assignments, contractor/employee agreements, confidentiality, privacy/security officers, and customer contracting authority.
- Appropriate cyber, technology E&O, CGL, D&O, and any industry-specific insurance.
- Payment, tax, auto-renewal, pricing, refunds, cancellation, consumer-protection, accessibility, language, anti-spam, and cookie/analytics obligations where applicable.
- U.S. expansion impacts before processing or marketing to U.S. customers.

## Release evidence counsel should receive

- Current architecture/data-flow and subprocessor inventory
- Security/incident/backup/restore and tenant-isolation test evidence
- Data inventory, purpose map, retention schedule, export/deletion workflow
- All customer-facing strings and screenshots for high-risk workflows
- AI provider/model governance, evaluation results, known limitations, and change-management records
- OEM/estimating access methods and contractual/licensing evidence
- Proposed marketing claims and supporting measurements

The release checklist cannot be marked complete by software alone; named accountable humans must record approval.
